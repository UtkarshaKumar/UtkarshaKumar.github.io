---
title: Copy the Rule, Not the Screen
date: 2026-08-16
---

Copy a feature that works somewhere else and it usually doesn't work for you. The reason is nearly always the same: you copied the part you could see. What makes most features work is a rule underneath them, and rules don't show up in screenshots.

Airbnb is the clearest example I know.

## The rule is usually about timing, order, or permission

Airbnb won't show you what a guest wrote about you until you've written about them. You each get [fourteen days after checkout](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/13), and both reviews stay hidden until both are in or the window closes.

That is the whole mechanism. Because you can't read theirs first, you can't match it. Nobody softens a review to avoid getting one back, so the ratings mean something.

Now think about copying Airbnb's reviews. You'd copy stars, photos, a text box, host replies. All of it visible, none of it the thing that works. The stars are the screen. The timing is the rule.

![The same review form under two publishing rules: sequential publishing lets each side react to the other, simultaneous publishing does not](img/review-timing-rule.svg)

## You can test which half you are holding

Describe the thing without naming the industry it came from.

Airbnb's rule survives that: hold two judgments until both are committed, then reveal them together. Nothing in that sentence is about travel, which is why the same rule works for sealed bids, blind peer review, and any performance review where the manager writes before reading yours.

If you can't describe it without saying hosts or listings or tickets, you're holding the screen.

## Cheap building removed the thing that used to stop you

This mistake used to correct itself. Building was slow, you had more ideas than engineers, and weak copies died before anyone had to argue against them.

That is ending. AI writes the prototype, the tests, and a growing share of the real thing. When building is nearly free, "someone else did this" becomes enough reason to ship. The failure won't look like failure. It looks like a team shipping constantly and getting nowhere.

## Two checks before you copy anything

![Two checks before copying a mechanism: does it cause the behaviour you want, and are its conditions true in your product](img/two-checks-before-copying.svg)

First, name the behaviour you want. Not the missing feature — the thing you want people to do more of. Airbnb needed strangers to trust each other enough to sleep in each other's houses, and every part of that review rule follows from it. Without that sentence, every mechanism looks equally worth having.

Second, check what the rule needs in order to work. Simultaneous reveal only matters where both sides review each other and both can retaliate. In a marketplace where only buyers rate sellers, it protects nobody; it's just a delay.

Those conditions are easy to miss, because a product that works has already met them. You see the rule running. You don't see what it is standing on.

Get either check wrong and you'll decide the rule was overrated. What you actually did was copy the screen.
